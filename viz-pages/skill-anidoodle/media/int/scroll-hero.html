How a page gets made

Every picture starts with a line.

Scroll down and this page will draw one of ours for you, the way an illustrator does: pencil first, then paint, then the line that holds it all together.

Scroll to draw. Scroll back to undraw.
Step one

Loose ovals, and a line through them.

Before anything has a face, it has a gesture. Two ovals for the head and body, a line for how he stands, a line for where the ground is. Nobody will notice these later. Everyone would notice without them.

Step two

A light pencil pass, head first.

The drawing goes down faintly, so it can still change its mind. Head first, because the face decides everything else; then down, one stroke at a time.

Step three

Washes, from where the brush lands.

Sky, warm light, grass, then the robot himself, back to front. Watercolour never quite fills its outline, and that is the charm.

Step four

The final line, over dry paint.

Full-weight graphite, head first again. This is the pass that makes it look finished, and the one that is hardest to fake.

Done
