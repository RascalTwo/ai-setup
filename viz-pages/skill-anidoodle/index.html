Chapter 1 · What it isHand-drawn art, written as code

What you're looking at: a tour of the /anidoodle Claude Code skill. Everything visual or audible on this page was made by anidoodle: the film below, all 31 style plates, the timelapses, the characters, the scores and the live web pieces. None of it came from an image generator or a download. One pure function paints every frame; another composes and synthesizes every audio sample. So the same source rebuilds the same pixels and the same sound on any machine, at any size and any length. Use ← → to move between the nine chapters, or press ▶ Tour.

Made for this page with anidoodle. showcaseTitle.ts is a 20-second "drawing" film, 1280×720, 600 frames on a 120 bpm grid. The pen writes the name, the capitals go down stroke by stroke, and six prints are set down one per beat. It passed the skill's own 14-check gate (chapter 8 shows where it failed first).

Chapter 2 · Asking for somethingSix questions, then it draws

anidoodle asks one question per turn, gives its recommendation first, and skips anything your request already answered. If you say “you pick”, it picks and tells you why in one line. The answers go into a short brief file before anything is drawn. Try it: change the answers below. The frame is drawn to the real aspect ratio, and you get the brief, the beat grid, the workflow it follows and the exact scaffold command.

Chapter 3 · The styles31 hands, each a different way of making marks

A style here is not a palette swap. Each hand changes the medium, the edge and the order of the marks: crayon skips the paper's valleys, riso overprints one ink drum at a time, a woodblock pulls the keyblock and then the colour blocks. Hover a tile to watch that plate draw itself. Click to open its recipe, then scrub the timelapse. The bar chart shows how many hands each family has, and clicking a bar filters the grid.

Each tile is the last frame of that style's draw film, rendered for this page with node tools/render.mjs <drawFilm> --out x.webm (480 px here; any size at full resolution). Other routes: “match my image” analyses your picture and grows the nearest plate into a new hand (chapter 4). “a house style” reloads one saved earlier from styles/house/.

Chapter 4 · Output kindsEleven kinds of thing, one engine

Every piece is a Film (meta, shots that tile the frames, an optional score), and a still is just a film one frame long. Each kind has its own workflow. The gate squares count the human approvals it spends, the teal bar shows typical length on a log scale, and the dots mark motion, sound and interactivity. Click a row to see a real example made by the skill, with its steps and a line you could say to ask for one.

Chapter 5 · CharactersOne character, identical in every hand

A character is built once, as a module: canon proportions, a 3D rig with joint limits, drawn views, palette roles and distinguishing features. Every shot and every style poses that one module, so Mira is the same girl in woodcut, pixel art or toy brick. A gate checks bone lengths, joint limits, five digits, feet on the floor and identity across styles (IoU > 0.8 for grid styles). Pick a sheet:

Chapter 6 · ScoresMusic as note data, then measured

anidoodle writes the notes itself, as bar-checked data (a bar that doesn't add up throws). It synthesizes every instrument and masters to a loudness target. No samples, no loops. Music gets a plan, never an adjective: a style, a mood per section, the notes, then measurements. Because the model can't hear, one human listens to 8 seconds before it ships. Click any dot to play the piece, rendered for this page. It's plotted by what music.mjs meter measured: note onsets per second → busier, spectral centroid → brighter.

Nothing playing · the red dot is the ghost fixture, a deliberately hated sustained drone that the “ghost” guard exists to reject.

What a score looks like

line(3, "C5:2 Bb4:.5 Ab4:.5 | Bb4:2 Eb4:1 | [Ab1 Eb2 Ab2]:3@0.8", { role: "melody", bpb: 3 })
Plan
sections, each with a mood (or a blend), key + mode, melody types, a dynamic arc and an ending
13 modes
major, aeolian, harmonic/melodic minor, dorian, phrygian, lydian, mixolydian, major/minor pentatonic, blues, whole-tone, locrian
9 melody types
arpeggio, stepwise, hook, ostinato, drone, call & response, counter-melody, sequence, theme transformation
Instruments
modal piano, music box, bell, celesta, marimba, vibes, harp and guitar (Karplus-Strong), strings, FM bell, e-piano, chip pulse/triangle/noise, kick, snare, hat, bass, vinyl
Any length
fitToDuration drops optional sections, restates repeatable ones and moves the tempo within the style's range
Measured
integrated LUFS, LRA, true peak, onsets/s, centroid; guards for ghost, reverb, masking and plan (the declared mode matches the notes)
Mastering
gentle styles: −16 LUFS, static gain, never a compressor · dense styles: −14 LUFS through a look-ahead limiter

Chapter 7 · Interactive web piecesArt that reacts to your UI, live

An interactive piece is a live canvas in your page, not a video. It reacts to pointer, hover, focus, clicks, scroll, typing (length and caret only, never the text) and UI state (idle, busy, success, error). Every input goes into a 60 Hz log, and a frame is a pure function of that log, so any session can be replayed and hashed exactly. These three demos were emitted for this page with emit-interactive.mjs and each passed 11 of 11 checks. Go on, use them.

Chapter 8 · The engineDeterministic, gated, any size

The engine is a portable art core that knows nothing about the DOM, plus the tooling around it. The contract is absolute: renderFrame(frame, env) is pure, randomness comes only from rng(seed), and nothing reads a clock, uses ctx.filter or loads a file. That's why a piece can render on four workers, backwards, on another backend, and still come out hash-identical. Click a tool to see its flags.

Chapter 9 · The craft bar and the recapWould a proud illustrator ship this?

The engine is the easy half. Work gets thrown away because it had no point, because the subject was an icon of itself, because a style was only a palette swap, because the music had no form, or because the person spent their patience approving fragments. The skill carries the craft that prevents each of those.

Every piece has a point.A still: one idea, one focal subject, one light. A film: one transformation, one payoff, one token that returns.
State the realism.Name the anatomy, the view and the reference opened, for animals, objects and people alike.
A style is a way of making marks.Change the medium, the edge and the order of the marks, not just the palette.
Prove the look on ONE still, then build end to end.After that, approval gates cost more than they save.
The contract is absolute.Pure in (frame, env), rng(seed) only, no clock, no ctx.filter, nothing generated or downloaded. Your own images may be embedded, pinned by hash.
Music gets a plan, never an adjective.Style, a mood per section, notes as data, then measurements, then one human listens.
One directs, one builds, both write everything down.When a session degrades, reset: the files are the memory.

The tells it hunts for and fixes

subjects built from ellipses (an icon of a pear, not a pear)
one style recoloured into another
things that fade in instead of being drawn
people with rubber joints, mitten hands, floating feet
a character drawn differently from shot to shot
flat colour fields, same-weight strokes, evenly stamped scatter
gradient skies, oval bokeh, decoration without a reason
dead air: something must visibly change every second

Honest limits

It can't hear the score, watch the film move or use the interactive piece. Every claim about them comes from a measurement or from a human, and it tells you which. A green check is a claim until it's re-run.
