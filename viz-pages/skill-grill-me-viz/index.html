↓  WHY IT EXISTS

Words are a bad way to ask about a screen

A terminal interview asks "should it chime, speak, or flash text?" about a sound you can't hear, and "stacked pin or fanned pin?" about a shape you can't see. You answer from imagination, the agent builds from your imagined answer, and three hours later you meet the real thing and it is wrong. The cheapest moment to be wrong was the question.

BEFORE · A GRILL IN THE TERMINAL

Q6. How does it tell you it heard you?
A) chime B) says "on it" C) visual D) …
You pick a letter for something you have never heard. The tradeoff between "fast but silent" and "talks over the meeting" is a sentence, not an experience.

AFTER · THE SAME QUESTION ON A PAGE

▶ A  ▶ B  ▶ D  ▶ E (each one plays, with a timer)
The question carries its own prototype. You press play four times, then click. The exhibits below are real questions asked this way.

grill-me-viz keeps the discipline of grilling (one question at a time, a recommendation with its reason, walk the decision tree) and adds the medium: a page that stays open for the whole session, hot-reloads as the agent writes it, and records what you click and what you say. It was built to be used heavily, and it has been:

How these were counted

A script walked every grill-* folder of the viz library, read-only. Grills = folders (38; 35 have a recorded log). Questions = distinct Qn numbers per page, from each question's number badge or its data-pick="Qn:…", summed across pages (620). Rounds = distinct data-round values per page. From each page's .viz-data/feedback.jsonl (older pages: grill.jsonl): picks = lines with "type":"pick" (433; 429 distinct questions were answered at least once by a click, the rest by voice or terminal), spoken reactions = "type":"speech" lines (2,941, adding up their secs gives 235 minutes and about 35,000 words), Sends = "type":"send" lines. Voice lines are what the browser's speech model heard, mistakes included. Raw logs, screenshots and page sources stay private: everything below is re-staged with invented data.

Say it, and a page opens

Triggers: "grill me visually", "grill me viz" (or "grill me vis"), or any grilling question that can't be settled in words: how a screen looks, one layout or three. The standing rule is in AGENTS.md §3: before finalizing a plan, run grill-me-viz, and plain grilling only where viz can't run.

  • You get one URL. The tab stays open all session and hot-reloads as the agent adds each round. If the tab isn't focused, the favicon turns orange and the title reads "● Your turn".
  • Click an option. Picking highlights it, then folds the question to one line; clicking the line reopens it.
  • Or talk while you point. The pill in the corner starts the mic; each stretch of speech is anchored to whatever the pointer is over (a question card reads [Q3]). Alt-click types a line instead.
  • Send round N when you're done. Answering in the terminal still works and wins for the same question.

It asks. It does not decide, and it needs the live server.

  • Without live viz, the buttons save nothing. The skill needs viz in full mode (live URL, viz 9.4 or newer, for the page data route). In fallback mode the pick and Send buttons are decoration and answers come by terminal only, and the skill says so out loud.
  • Voice has a toll. The mic needs Chrome with WebGPU, and the first mic click on a machine downloads a speech model of about 2.4 GB (cached after). Say so on the first session. Typing always works: Alt-click anything.
  • A plain question stays plain. A naming question or a yes/no is text. A visual is added only where it adds value; a prototype for its own sake is padding.
  • The page is not published. Grill pages are tagged grill-session (hidden from the library's default views), and the data route exists only on the local server. The exhibits on this page are re-staged precisely because the originals are private.
  • It is not the decision. The .rec box says why the recommended option is recommended; the pick is always yours, and a terminal reply overrides the page for the same question. Several exhibits above are the user overruling the recommendation.
  • Questions are still grilling's job. The order, the one-at-a-time discipline and when the session is done belong to grilling; this skill is only how the two meet.

Where it lives and what it keeps

The skill skills/grill-me-viz/: SKILL.md (the seven steps), grill.ts (the page script; grill.js is its generated copy for fallback mode, from bun run sync:kit in the viz skill), grill.css and grill-expand.js. Copied beside each new page's index.html.
Declared kind: skill, state: false. No integrates, no requires, no deletion-policy: the dependencies are in the prose.
Its state None of its own. The skill keeps nothing between runs. A session's answers live in the page it made, in the viz library under grill-<topic>/ with .viz-data/feedback.jsonl (git-ignored, never published). That log is viz's state, not this skill's, and it is the only record of the spoken half of a grill: delete it and the picks survive as the page's decided lines, the spoken reactions do not.
Requires Step 1 loads two skills through the Skill tool: grilling, then viz, in its full mode. The mic needs Chrome with WebGPU; typed answers need nothing beyond viz itself.
Called from AGENTS.md §3 (Planning & Verification): "Grill visually: before finalizing a plan, run grill-me-viz." It is a standing rule rather than something the model is trusted to reach for. It also builds on viz's feedback widget, which owns the pill, the mic and the log.

What grill.ts does on the page

grill.ts is deliberately small and does four jobs. Fold: mark() flags the pick among its siblings and writes the answer into the card's header, fold() collapses the body after a 450 ms beat so you see the highlight; wrapping each card's body in .body > div once lets the height animate. Attention: the favicon says whose turn it is (orange "?" a round you haven't seen, blue pencil your turn, grey "…" sent, Claude's turn). Restore: on load it fetches _log/feedback and replays picks oldest-first, so the latest per question ends up marked and a hot reload never loses an answer. Self-check: window.grill.tinyText() lists text in the newest round rendering under 11 px; the agent must get [] before handing a round over, then look at a screenshot too. grill-expand.js adds the top-right Expand all / Collapse all button.

Same action, two buttons. A round's data-send button and the widget pill's Send call the same feedback.send, which waits for speech still being transcribed first. The grill that designed the widget kept it that way on purpose: the more branching code we write, the more we have to maintain.
