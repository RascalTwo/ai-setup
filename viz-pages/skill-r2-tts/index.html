Four tools each picked their own voice

Text to speech (TTS) is a program that reads written words aloud. Before r2-tts, speech came from four separate places that each chose their own voice and their own engine (an engine is the program that actually makes the sound). voicemode, which lets Joseph talk to the agent, went through a small router that tried a GPU on the home PC and then the Mac. The viz and pr-viz films carried their own copy of the Mac engines. demo-viz read its cards with the browser's built-in voice. anidoodle had a narration tool of its own.

Nothing said whose voice a tool should use, and a fix to one engine never reached the others. In blind listening tests (nobody told which clip was which), Joseph's own cloned voice beat every stock voice. But hearing your own voice answer you in a conversation is odd, while a demo for someone else sounds best in your voice. So the choice stopped being "which engine" and became "who is listening".

Say who is listening

A tool runs r2_tts.py say with an audience, some text and an output file. It prints one line of JSON per file written, naming the voice and the engine that made it.

R2TTS=~/.claude/skills/r2-tts/r2_tts.py
python3 $R2TTS say --audience others --text "…" --out clip.mp3
python3 $R2TTS say --audience both --lean me --text-file s.txt --out clip.wav # clip.me.wav (the default) and clip.others.wav
voicemode me: its replies come back in Harper.
grill and spec films me: they are for Joseph.
demo-viz both --lean me: voice.ts pre-renders every story line in both voices, and the 🗣 toggle swaps between them.
pr-viz both --lean others: through the engine "r2-tts" in ~/.config/viz/voices.json.
anidoodle others: films go to other people (provider r2tts).
  • Emotion (--emotion happy|sad|angry|fear|disgust|surprise): the chain starts at Ming for either voice: in an A/B, Ming's happy beat Zonos's emotion steering. If Ming is away, the rest of the chain speaks the line plainly.
  • Speed (--speed): Qwen3 and Ming speak at it natively; the other engines are retimed with ffmpeg's atempo.
  • Pronunciations: the TTS <regex> <replacement> lines in ~/.voicemode/voicemode.env apply to every request and are re-read each time (kubectl becomes "kube control").
  • Check: python3 -B test_r2_tts.py in skills/r2-tts/.

What it won't do

  • Joseph's voice can be slow to start. The first engine, Zonos3-Mini, takes about 50 seconds to load cold, then about 1.5 seconds for each 11 seconds of speech. It needs about 6.7 GB of the Mac's memory while loaded and is stopped after 5 idle minutes.
  • It doesn't stream. The whole clip is made first, then returned in one piece.
  • Only Ming does tags, pitch and volume. Inline tags such as [Laughter], plus pitch and volume, start the chain at Ming on the home PC. Every other engine gets the text with the tags stripped out. Emotion above 2.0 broke Zonos takes in testing, so it stays at 2.0.
  • Question tags are slow on Ming. [Question-ei] and its siblings often run on past the end of the sentence. They needed 2 to 3 re-rolls in testing, which is 15 to 25 seconds.
  • The laugh isn't his. Joseph's reference clip contains no laugh, so Ming's [Laughter] doesn't sound like him.
  • A home PC that failed stays "down" for up to 30 seconds before it is tried again, so a powered-off PC costs about one second, not a hang.
  • Ming ignores the shared GPU lock. Take gpu.lock yourself before heavy GPU work on the PC.
  • No licence gating. r2-tts does not check whose voice you are asking for; Joseph picks voices himself.
  • The anidoodle hook sits in a third-party skill. An update of that skill may overwrite it.

Where it lives

Integrates with voicemode (VOICEMODE_TTS_BASE_URLS=http://127.0.0.1:8895/v1), launchd (the job com.rascaltwo.r2-tts), Ollama (phonon, which speaks Harper), viz (the engine "r2-tts" in ~/.config/viz/voices.json) and Windows Task Scheduler (ming-tts-server on the home PC, at logon). demo-viz and anidoodle call say directly.
Requires python3, ffmpeg and uv; by hand, Zonos3-Mini in ~/.agents/state/r2-tts/zonos3, Qwen3-TTS through mlx-audio, and Ming-3.0-tts on the home PC (its setup is skills/r2-tts/ming/README.md). The Mac side is macOS; the Ming server runs on Windows 10 with WSL2 and an NVIDIA GPU.
Its state ~/.agents/state/r2-tts/: serve/ (the copy of r2_tts.py that launchd actually runs, because launchd can't read ~/Desktop), serve.log, zonos3/ (the Zonos server) and the blind*/ listening-test clips behind the ranking. On the PC, a folder such as ai-eval\ming-server\ holds the Ming server, the reference clip with its transcript, and server.log.
Safe to delete? Not serve/ or zonos3/, deletion-policy: retain: removing serve/ makes the launchd job fail until the script is copied back, and zonos3/ is the engine itself. The log and the blind*/ clips can go; the clips are only the evidence for the ranking.

One request, end to end

A tool runs r2_tts.py say --audience others --text "…" --out clip.mp3. Joseph's voice has the longest chain, so it is the one to follow.

  1. Find the daemon. speak_one() checks GET /health on 127.0.0.1:8895. If serve answers, it posts the request to it, so Zonos and Qwen3 stay loaded between calls. If not, or if R2TTS_LOCAL=1, it runs the engines in its own process and stops any model server it started when it exits.
  2. Read the request. do_POST takes POST /v1/audio/speech. request_from_json() turns audience into a voice (me is Harper, others is Joseph). With no audience, as from voicemode, the voice name decides: joseph, or a body carrying ref_audio, is Joseph; a Ming built-in such as US_James is that voice; anything else, such as af_sky, is Harper.
  3. Rewrite the text. synthesize() applies the pronunciation rules from voicemode.env, then refuses if nothing is left to say once tags are removed.
  4. Walk the chain. For each engine in Req.chain(), in order, it hands over the text (tags stripped for everyone but Ming). The first engine to return audio wins; an exception is logged to Req.tries and the next engine runs. If every engine fails the request ends in a 502.
  5. Zonos and the re-roll guard. zonos() starts the Zonos server from ~/.agents/state/r2-tts/zonos3 if it isn't up, and sends the text with the reference clip ~/.config/viz/voice-references/joseph-animated.wav at delivery.tempo 2. Then good_take() measures the audio: a length outside 0.4 to 2.5 times the expected (about 19 characters a second) or any pause over 1 second is a bad take. It re-rolls the seed (7, 11, 23, 42, so up to 4 tries) before the chain moves on to Qwen3.
  6. Ming's own guard. ming() first asks PcHealth, a one-second probe cached for 30 seconds. On the PC ming_server.py loads the model on the first request (about 12 seconds cold, 2 warm) and checks its own takes, with up to 5 re-rolls before it answers 502 and the chain moves on. It unloads after 10 idle minutes, freeing about 3 GB of video memory.
  7. Level and encode. finish() runs ffmpeg once: atempo if the engine couldn't honour the speed, then loudnorm=I=-16, so a quiet Zonos take (about 6 dB below the rest) and a louder Ming take land at the same loudness, then the file format. say writes the file by its extension and prints {"file", "audience", "voice", "engine"}; the daemon returns the same audio with X-Voice-Backend naming the engine.
  8. Stay warm, then let go. A reaper thread in serve wakes every 30 seconds and stops any model server it started once it has sat idle for 5 minutes.
  9. Keep the daemon running. launchd runs the job com.rascaltwo.r2-tts from the installed copy. After editing r2_tts.py, copy it to ~/.agents/state/r2-tts/serve/ and run launchctl kickstart -k gui/$(id -u)/com.rascaltwo.r2-tts.

The measured numbers

These come from the Ming evaluation, run before r2-tts: the same six sentences rendered from Joseph's reference clip by Ming on the home PC and by Qwen3 on a heavily loaded Mac, so Qwen3's times are not a clean benchmark. Joseph picked Ming on every sentence.

Short casual line Ming 3.7 s Qwen3 22.1 s
Technical sentence Ming 8.6 s Qwen3 28.7 s
Line with a laugh tag Ming 4.7 s Qwen3 31.7 s
20 second paragraph Ming 30.8 s Qwen3 78.4 s
  • Ming's model: about 3 GB of video memory after load, a peak of 3.4 GB reserved.
  • Ming's retries: of 28 tag, emotion and speed demos, 11 needed a re-roll. The four [Question-*] tags needed 3 each; most failures were runaways to the 20 second ceiling, one was a 0.3 second early stop.
  • Zonos against Qwen3: in the third blind test Zonos won 3 to 1 (Qwen3 had won the second), and again in the labelled rematch once the gap guard was in. That is why Zonos is first for Joseph's voice. The ranking and the clips are in docs/adr/2026-11-20-pick-the-voice-by-who-hears-it.md.
