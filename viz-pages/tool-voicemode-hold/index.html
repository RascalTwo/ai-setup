The problem

Driving an agent by voice is pleasant right up to the moment it says something wrong and keeps going. The only interrupt that existed was voicemode control skip-forward on prefix+m, which kills the utterance and ends the turn. That's the right tool when the whole reply is a dead end. It is the wrong tool for "no, not that table" — you lose the other nine-tenths of a good answer to correct one clause.

So there needed to be a way to open a gap: stop the voice where it is, say the thing, and let it resume from the same word. That's prefix+j.

The two keys

prefix+m  — ends it

skip-forward over the control socket. The utterance is cut, the turn is over, the mic opens and a reply is expected.

prefix+j  — holds it

Pause. The mic opens, the pane freezes on the exact word. Press again and the voice carries on from there. The turn never ended.

What it doesn't do

  • It cannot change the sentence it interrupted. The agent's speech is already-generated text by the time you hear it, so an interjection cannot alter what the rest of the utterance says — only what comes after it. This is accepted, not solved, and it's the honest ceiling on the whole idea.
  • Nothing is listening before first audio. Press the key in the gap between the tool call and the first sound and there is no socket bound yet; the hold is not delivered, and the pane deliberately doesn't freeze.
  • herdr or tmux for the pane, herdr for the key.

The binding runs the capture under voice-mode's own interpreter, not system python3: it needs sounddevice and numpy, and that venv is the one place they are guaranteed, because voicemode records with them.

Nothing here patches voicemode. It's a uv tool install, so an upgrade would wipe any edit made inside it — all of this lives in hooks and config that belong to me.

Its own state is at ~/.agents/state/voicemode-hold/, not in ~/.voicemode/. It used to be in there, and the old name said why that was a problem: this tool was called voicemode too. Two owners writing into one directory means nobody can say which files are safe to delete. What still points at voice-mode's directory is exactly what voice-mode owns — the control socket and the event log.

The run-up, and why it isn't a timestamp

An interjection is useless on its own. "No, that's wrong" has no referent: the agent generated the text but never heard it spoken, so it has no idea which part was in the air when you objected. So every interjection carries a run-up — the last 18 words that had actually been spoken at the moment the key went down.

A timestamp would not do the job, which is the question worth asking and I asked it. The word-level timings live in the pane, not in the transcript the agent can see; "at 0:41" means nothing to something that has no clock over its own speech.

Getting it back to the agent

There is exactly one channel. You speak long after the tool call went out, so nothing is still listening on the way in — the interjection rides back out on the PostToolUse hook's hookSpecificOutput.additionalContext, ahead of the hook's own return.

PreToolUse   mcp__voicemode__converse  →  transcript.py prime   # pane gets the text
                                             # ── speech plays, you press prefix+j ──
PostToolUse  mcp__voicemode__converse  →  transcript.py heard   # drains interjections
                                          └─ hookSpecificOutput.additionalContext

Why it writes the socket directly

Pause and resume go straight down voicemode's control socket at ~/.voicemode/control.sock — newline-delimited JSON, bound only while the server is speaking. The obvious implementation, shelling out to the voicemode CLI, was built first and thrown away.

The CLI cost most of a second of Python startup at each end of a hold. The pane measures hold length to shift its karaoke clock, so that startup showed up on screen as the highlight drifting behind the voice. A latency bug you can see is a latency bug you have to fix.

voicemode logs no pause/resume event at all, so TTS_FIRST_AUDIO and TTS_PLAYBACK_END under ~/.voicemode/logs/events/ are the only clock there is. The transcript's own t0 leads real audio by the MCP round trip and can't be used.

How the pane behaves

It has exactly one live repaint region, and it exists only while speech is actually moving. Everything else is ordinary terminal scrollback.

  • A finished utterance is released — printed once unwindowed, then never repainted. That's what puts it in scrollback where you can read back whatever prefix+m cut off.
  • The reply prints under the utterance it answers, and a new utterance doesn't inherit the last reply. Otherwise the scrollback reads backwards and shows every reply twice.
  • A hold does the same thing: prints the whole utterance and leaves it alone. This is the answer to "I want to read ahead without being made to talk" — prefix+m opens the mic and demands a reply, prefix+j just stops the world.
  • The live window sits a quarter down rather than centred. A pane can't scroll its own viewport, so while an utterance is taller than the pane a window is unavoidable — and the words worth showing are the ones still coming.
