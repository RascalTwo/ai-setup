Nothing spoke in Joseph's voice quickly

voicemode spoke with Kokoro's af_sky. pr-viz could clone Joseph with Qwen3-TTS, but only on the Mac, where a clip took 22 to 78 seconds on a busy machine. There was no good copy of Joseph's voice that both tools could reach.

The home PC has a GPU that sits idle. Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B runs in about 3.4 GB of it. Joseph listened to Ming and Qwen3 render the same six sentences from the same reference clip and picked Ming on every one.

You just talk to voicemode

  • voicemode has VOICEMODE_TTS_BASE_URLS set to http://127.0.0.1:8895/v1 first and Kokoro second. Its voice stays af_sky; the router turns any voice but joseph into Ming's US_Emma.
  • pr-viz uses it through "url": "http://127.0.0.1:8895" on the joseph-animated profile. Any request carrying ref_audio is Joseph.
  • By hand: POST /v1/audio/speech with voice joseph, or a built-in (US_Emma, US_James, US_Stella, UK_George, UK_Charlotte).
  • Optional fields for Joseph: emotion (happy, sad, angry, fear, disgust, surprise, neutral), pitch and volume (low, medium, high), and a speed under 0.9 or over 1.1. Inline tags such as [Laughter] or [Sigh] pass straight through.
  • Pronunciations: the TTS <regex> <replacement> lines in ~/.voicemode/voicemode.env apply to every request, so pr-viz gets them too (kubectl becomes "kube control").
  • Check: python3 tools/ming-tts/test_ming_tts.py.

What it won't do

  • Question tags are slow. [Question-ei] and its siblings often run on past the end of the sentence. They needed 2 to 3 re-rolls in testing, which is 15 to 25 seconds.
  • It doesn't stream. The whole clip comes back at once. A cold start, when the model loads on the first request, takes about 12 seconds.
  • Controls are Joseph-only. Emotion, pitch, volume and inline tags are not applied to the built-in voices; tags are stripped from their text.
  • The laugh isn't his. Joseph's reference clip contains no laugh, so [Laughter] doesn't sound like him.
  • A PC that failed stays "down" for up to 30 seconds before the router tries it again.
  • The server ignores the shared GPU lock. Take gpu.lock yourself before heavy GPU work on the PC.
  • pr-viz's clip cache is keyed on text and reference, not backend, so clips cached from Qwen3 stay until their text changes.

Where it lives

Integrates with voicemode (VOICEMODE_TTS_BASE_URLS), pr-viz (the joseph-animated profile url in ~/.config/viz/voices.json), launchd (com.rascaltwo.ming-router) and Windows Task Scheduler (ming-tts-server on the home PC, at logon).
Requires The home-pc skill, python3, ffmpeg and ssh; Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B in WSL2 on the PC, Kokoro on the Mac, and Qwen3-TTS through mlx-audio. Router: macOS. Server: Windows 10 with WSL2 and an NVIDIA GPU.
Its state ~/.agents/state/ming-tts/: router/router.py (the copy launchd actually runs, because launchd can't read ~/Desktop), router.log (backend and retries per request), test renders and the evaluation report. On the PC, ai-eval\ming-server\ holds the server, the reference clip with its transcript, and server.log.
Safe to delete? Not the installed copy, deletion-policy: retain: removing router/router.py makes the LaunchAgent fail until it is copied back. The log and test renders can go.

One request, end to end

voicemode sends POST /v1/audio/speech to router.py on 127.0.0.1:8895 with voice=af_sky, response_format=pcm.

  1. Rewrite the text. pronunciation_rules() re-reads the TTS lines of voicemode.env on every request, so edits apply live.
  2. Pick the voice. voice == "joseph" or a body with ref_audio is Joseph. A Ming built-in name passes through; anything else is US_Emma.
  3. Probe the PC. PcHealth.up() calls GET /health on 100.65.216.8:8894 with a 1 second timeout and caches the answer for 30 seconds, so a powered-off PC costs about one second, not a hang.
  4. Cross the network. Tailscale delivers to Windows port 8894. A netsh portproxy forwards it to 127.0.0.1:8893, which WSL2 already forwards into the VM. A firewall rule admits only 100.64.0.0/10.
  5. Choose the task. In ming_server.py, Ming.speak() maps Joseph to TTS (reference clip plus its transcript), EMOTION or BASIC, and a built-in voice to IP. The model loads on the first request.
  6. Check the take. expected_s() estimates 19 characters per second plus 0.8 s per tag. Decoding is capped at 2.6 times that (max_decode_steps), and a take shorter than 0.4 times or longer than 2.5 times the estimate is re-rolled with the next seed, up to 5 tries. Then it returns 502 and the router falls back.
  7. Return audio. Ming sends 44.1 kHz wav with X-Ming-Retries. The router runs ffmpeg for any other format (pcm is 24 kHz s16le mono) and adds X-Voice-Backend.
  8. Fallbacks. Joseph goes to Qwen.speak(), which starts mlx_audio.server on port 8892 with the joseph-animated profile and stops it after 5 idle minutes. Everyone else goes to Kokoro on port 8880 with af_sky.
  9. Free the GPU. A reaper thread in the server drops the model and calls torch.cuda.empty_cache() after 10 idle minutes (about 3 GB back).

The measured numbers

The same six sentences, rendered from Joseph's pr-viz reference clip by both engines. Ming ran on the home PC; Qwen3 ran on a heavily loaded Mac, so its times are not a clean benchmark.

Short casual line Ming 3.7 s Qwen3 22.1 s
Technical sentence Ming 8.6 s Qwen3 28.7 s
Line with a laugh tag Ming 4.7 s Qwen3 31.7 s
20 second paragraph Ming 30.8 s Qwen3 78.4 s
  • Model: about 3 GB of VRAM after load, a peak of 3.4 GB reserved.
  • Through the router: 1.8 to 3.5 s to the first audio when warm, 12.3 s cold.
  • Retries: of 28 tag, emotion and speed demos, 11 needed a re-roll. The four [Question-*] tags needed 3 each; most failures were runaways to the 20 second ceiling, one was a 0.3 second early stop.
  • Fallbacks: with the PC unreachable, Kokoro answered in 1.3 s and Joseph's Qwen3 clip in 2.6 s once its server was warm.
