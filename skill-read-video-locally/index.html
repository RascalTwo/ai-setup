Two half-senses, one timeline

A frame-by-frame native read of a video burns thousands of vision tokens. The local route costs ~0 — but no single local model does the whole job: Qwen3-VL (via mlx-vlm) sees and can't hear, Whisper hears and can't see. Feed Qwen3-VL the transcript as timestamped text and the two halves line up: "spoken X at 0:42" against "frame at 0:42 shows Y". A vision-only model then behaves like an audio+video one, locally, with no Omni model needed.

The hearing half isn't reimplemented here — transcription is delegated to the transcribe-media skill (MLX Whisper, GPU-accelerated), falling back to the openai-whisper CLI if that skill isn't installed. One speech-to-text engine, shared. Word-level timestamps are the point: they're what anchors deictic words like "here" and "this" to a moment.

The narration is the query list

In narrated mode you don't author questions — the speaker already did. Every comment in the voice-over becomes a claim verified against the video at its own timestamp. In silent mode (an agent's own screen capture, no audio) Whisper is skipped and your --question drives it, which upgrades self-verification from a flipbook of stills to motion over time: did it animate, or teleport? — a question one screenshot cannot answer.

Always pass a specific intent. Like read-image-locally, a video model is only as good as the prompt; never expect anything useful from a vague request. And subjective calls — "is the easing juicy, is it fun" — stay a human's, in both modes.

When the video path averages your answer away

The video path samples frames as a temporal sequence and merges adjacent pairs, so a brief event — a fast tween, a six-frame transition — buried in a mostly-static clip gets averaged into a gist. The model reports a flat "it's a green plate" and misses the change entirely. --montage reframes the temporal question as a spatial one: tile the sampled frames into one numbered contact sheet and read it in image mode, which Qwen3-VL resolves far better than a fast transition. It auto-trims the settled tail by frame-diff, so the brief transition fills the grid instead of getting two tiles while ten are wasted on a static end.

# the model returns a per-tile 0–10 trajectory, never a verdict
snap  → 0,0,0,0,0,3,6,9,10,10,10,10
tween → 0,0,0,3,5,6,7,8,9,10,10,10
# the prompt never says "snap" or "gradual" — so the model can't be led.

You never pick the sampler

Uniform --fps sampling over-samples a static screencast — six hundred near-identical slides — and under-samples a fast cut. Frame selection is a different job from frame reading, so it's delegated to claude-real-video: deterministic ffmpeg-and-Pillow frame math, no model, no API, scene-change selection plus dedup. It picks; Qwen3-VL and Whisper still read and hear. The caller picks nothing.

clip ≥ 20s, crv present  → crv        scene-selected + deduped
clip < 20s               → uniform    # a correctness call, not a speed one
crv missing or failing   → uniform + a warning naming the downgrade
answer degenerates       → the other one, automatically

Short clips stay uniform on purpose. crv's whole job is deleting near-duplicate frames — and a smooth tween is a run of near-duplicates, so dedup would eat the very frames that answer "did it slide or teleport?". The real routing axis is the question, not the clip; sniffing the question is brittle, so duration stands in for it — agent self-captures are short and motion-shaped, real-world video is long and content-shaped.

Two measured degenerations, one detector. Handed too many dense stills, the model loops one caption instead of reading each frame: first the same sentence for 14 consecutive frames, then later "similar to the previous one but a different layout" four times running. So crv's frames are read as numbered contact sheets, not loose stills — and the detector compares consecutive lines for near-identity, because an exact-match check sails straight past the second failure.

Boundaries, measured

  • Use it for "how many in-between states were there" — position over time, presence, a scalar that ramps. For easing-feel, continuity or bounce, the still grid hides it: use the video path, or a human.
  • The model smears a hard edge by ~2–3 tiles, reading an instant jump as a short ramp. Separating a true snap from a sub-0.2s tween is therefore marginal — raise --tiles and judge on rise-width, not on "any intermediate value".
  • For a pure colour or brightness trajectory, a direct pixel read (PIL over the frames) beats any model. Reach for the model when the per-frame question is semantic — where, what, whether — not a number you could just measure.
  • Montage needs Pillow for the sheet build.

The lever is prompt framing, not model size

Narrated verification is strong and stable — the transcript anchors the model. Motion was the hard part, and the fix wasn't a bigger model. A plain "does it slide or teleport?" made both the 4-bit and the 8-bit model lazily call a smooth slide a teleport. Enumeration framing — walk the frames one by one, note each position, judge afterwards — fixes it, and is now baked into the silent-question prompt.

The 8-bit default earns its place in combination with that framing, not on its own: asked to enumerate, 4-bit sometimes degenerated into repeated junk (x=10, x=10, …) while 8-bit enumerates cleanly every time. Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-8bit is ~10 GB; bf16 isn't worth it, since 8-bit is near-lossless and faster on Apple Silicon where inference is bandwidth-bound.

ollama's qwen3-vl:8b is image-only. Its runner does no temporal video encoding — don't route video through ollama. mlx-vlm is the video path. (ollama stays fine for single images and read-image-locally.)

It is allowed to fail

On any hard failure — mlx/Qwen3-VL load error, empty output — the engine prints RVL_FAILED … and exits non-zero. That's a designed exit, not a dead end: extract frames with ffmpeg and read them natively. The Whisper step is separately best-effort — if it fails, the run simply continues in silent mode and warns on stderr.

ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -vf fps=2 /tmp/frames/f%03d.png   # then Read the frames

The same trick makes a clip in the first place, with no new dependency: drive a sequence through the app's screenshot hook, then ffmpeg -framerate 10 -pattern_type glob the burst into an mp4 and question it. That works even where OS screen recording is blocked for the app — a Godot window driven by computer-use, for instance.
