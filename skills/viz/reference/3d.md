# 3D in a film — what renders, what breaks

Read this before putting three.js in a `film()` viz. The contract is in `timeline.md` ("Canvas /
WebGL inside a film()"); this is what building four review films on it taught. Design lessons —
when 3D earns its place at all — are in pr-viz's `reference/3d.md`.

## Pick the renderer by what has to be read

- **Code, diffs, anything the kit already styles → `CSS3DRenderer`.** Put the real DOM in space.
  Syntax highlighting, `data-b` fades, `data-h` highlights and pointer boxes all keep working,
  because they're still elements the film drives. Re-drawing code onto a canvas texture lost
  every one of those.
- **Objects, volumes, glow → `WebGLRenderer`**, via `kit/stage3d.js`: camera stops keyed to
  narration lines, `plate()` for text on a surface, orbit and per-stop replay in review.
- **Names that must read at any distance → `CSS2DRenderer`**: screen-size labels pinned to 3D
  points. Lane and district names in an overview were unreadable as 3D text and fine as CSS2D.
- Mixed is fine: WebGL city underneath, CSS3D panels on top, one camera.

## It renders

- `viz render`'s headless Chrome gets **WebGL2 on the real GPU with no extra flags** (tested on an
  Apple M3). **WebGPU has no adapter there**, so use `WebGLRenderer`.
- Draw only on `viz-frame`, from `t`: `start(b)` gives beat times, and camera and motion are
  pure functions of them. No rAF, no `performance.now`, no unseeded random. The render splits
  frames across browsers, so anything carried between frames breaks at the joins.
- The seam warning ("seek(t) is not idempotent") showed up on scenes that *were* idempotent. The
  check compares PNGs byte for byte, and at the joins the mp4 differed by ~30 of 2M pixels, which
  is encoder noise. To judge a seam, diff the mp4 frames either side (`magick compare -fuzz 8%`)
  instead of trusting the warning.
- A three.js film with a city of WebGL buildings rendered in about 25 minutes. Budget for it.

## Traps that cost a round each

- **CSS3D has no near plane.** An element the camera has flown past draws huge and mirrored
  across the frame. Hide what's behind the camera
  (`o.visible = o.position.z < cam.z - 400`), and fade out earlier panels that stand between the
  camera and the current one.
- **CSS3DRenderer only attaches an element on its first visible render.** film.js scans for
  `data-b`/`data-h` when `rescan()` runs, and a panel not yet in the page is never animated:
  it stays invisible and its highlights never play. Render once at setup, with everything
  visible, *before* `f.rescan()`.
- **An invalid CSS transform is dropped silently.** A NaN camera position doesn't throw. The
  camera element just loses its transform and the scene goes blank. When everything vanishes,
  check the maths (e.g. `xs[-1]` from a one-element list).
- **Cloning after the scan is fine only if you clone before `rescan()`.** Copies of a panel
  (same code in three modules) get their own highlights because film scans them afterwards.
- A dark label on a dark scene disappears. Put the name *on* the object (a texture on the
  cylinder, the icon on the block's face), not on a floating plate above it.

## Readable, and in frame

- Measure, don't guess: project every object's corners with the camera at each stop and at
  mid-moves (`new THREE.Vector3(...).project(cam)`), and require |x| < 0.95 and y > -0.6, so
  nothing sits under the captions. Loop distance (and aim) until it fits. The fitted overview
  came from exactly this search.
- Text on the thing being read: ≥ ~16px at 1080p. For a panel of width w, the camera distance
  that fits it is `w / 2 / (tan(fov/2) · aspect)`. Three 1480px panels side by side read at ~7px,
  so don't frame them together. Hold on each instead (pr-viz `reference/3d.md`).
- A move lands near-flat over whatever the narration asks the viewer to read. Mid-move, back off
  and rise so the camera passes over things rather than through them.
- Blur (depth of field) and fog read as "unfinished", not "deep". Depth came from a staircase
  (layers rising toward the back), thick slabs, a floor grid, and the camera moving forward
  through it.

## Real assets beat primitives

- Screenshots of the real page (captured through the user's browser, read-only) played as a
  frame sequence picked from `t`. Never an animated GIF: it plays on the wall clock, not `t`.
- Official icons (e.g. the AWS Architecture Icons package) extruded with `SVGLoader` +
  `ExtrudeGeometry` read as the real service. Keep the assets in the viz's own folder and list
  them in `viz:bundle`.
