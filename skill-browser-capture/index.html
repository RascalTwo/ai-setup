A slideshow, or no session

Putting a browser flow into a doc, a PR or a walkthrough has two obvious routes and both fail in a way you only notice at the end. The GIF recorder produces a slideshow, always — one frame per action — so the "choppy GIF" you keep re-recording is the tool working as designed. And a spawned headless browser records beautifully but arrives at the login page with no session, which for anything behind SSO is the end of the exercise.

What was missing was the third thing: recording the browser the user is already signed into, at full frame rate, without asking them to click anything or hand over a password. That is what this skill is, and the whole design falls out of one Chrome precondition and one very literal error message.

Pick the route before you touch anything

Turning a browser flow into files you can embed has three routes, and they are in strict order of preference. The reason the skill opens with the choice rather than a procedure: every obvious shortcut here fails silently, which is how this costs hours instead of minutes.

  • Route 1 — MCP capture, the user's own Chrome. The default; prefer always. Real login/SSO, real data, real device pixel ratio. About a second of human-visible disruption and zero clicks from them. Nothing else can do that.
  • Route 2 — a spawned browser (record-flow.js). No session at all, but it never touches the screen. For when Route 1 is impossible: no GUI, CI or background, the screen must not be disturbed, or the choreography has to be deterministic and scripted.
  • Route 3 — the GIF recorder (gif_creator). Only when a click-annotated how-to GIF is specifically what's wanted and smooth motion isn't needed.

You can always convert a Route 1 or Route 2 video to GIF with ffmpeg — which usually looks better than the GIF recorder anyway. The skill fires on "add screenshots of the steps", "show how to do X in the UI", "record a walkthrough", "make a video of this" — and especially on "this GIF came out choppy".

The hard line

Route 1 wins because the MCP is the user's real browser, so their sessions are already there — a protected app typically shows an account picker with accounts already marked "Signed in", one click and you're in, no password. Never type the user's credentials. If a real password field appears, stop and hand back to the human. The skill calls that a hard line, not a difficulty.

The GIF trap. gif_creator yields a slideshow, always — one frame per action, and extra screenshot calls add zero frames. If a GIF looks janky or choppy, that is not a quality bug to iterate on; it is Route 3 working exactly as designed. Switch routes, don't try harder.
  • Route 1 takes over the screen for about a second. A red overlay, and Chrome pulled to the front. It cannot be done invisibly; that is what Route 2 is for.
  • It records a tab, not a desktop. preferCurrentTab is the reason there's no picker, and the cost is that nothing outside Chrome is in frame.
  • Switching to a different Chrome tab mid-capture is unverified. Occlusion by another app is proven safe; tab-switching is probably fine and has simply never been tested, so the skill tells you to tell the user not to.
  • Route 2 has no session and cannot get one. Copying cookies won't ride an SSO session — that dead end is written down so nobody re-derives it.
  • It records; it does not edit. Trimming, captions burn-in and GIF conversion are ffmpeg afterwards, not features here.

Where it lives

The kitassets/mcp-recorder.js (injected, becomes window.__cap), scripts/raise-chrome.sh (the AppleScript that satisfies Chrome's precondition), and scripts/record-flow.js for Route 2.
IntegratesChrome, through Chrome MCP. Route 1 has no other moving part — the recording is done by the page itself, by getDisplayMedia and MediaRecorder.
Requiresffmpeg (verify and convert), chrome + claude-in-chrome (the MCP connection), and node for Route 2. On macOS, raise-chrome.sh also needs osascript and Chrome automation permission.
Hard dependencyui-narration. record-flow.js injects it unless you pass --no-kit, and exits with the paths it tried if the skill isn't installed rather than recording a silent, unannotated take.
Its stateNone. The manifest says state: false and the skill keeps nothing between runs — no recording index, no settings file, no remembered tab or URL, nothing under ~/.agents/. window.__cap lives in the page and dies with the tab; the injection is per-tab and has to be redone next time.
What it writesFiles in ~/Downloads/, put there by Chrome's own download mechanism via a real <a download> click — the video, and a sibling <name>.vtt when the flow called __narrate.say(). That is the output you asked for; nothing is hidden.
Safe to deleteEverything it produced. Re-running re-records. There is no local state that a deletion could corrupt.

The one constraint, and why it is not about headlessness

All three must hold at the instant capture starts, and only then: the target tab is the selected tab in its window, its window is frontmost and not occluded by another app, and a trusted click lands within about five seconds — the MCP computer tool's clicks are trusted. Miss any one and getDisplayMedia rejects.

InvalidStateError: Invalid state means one thing: the tab wasn't visible. macOS marks a Chrome page hidden when its window is occluded by another app — so an editor sitting on top is enough, even though the tab is selected and the page is "open". scripts/raise-chrome.sh is the fix, and it is exactly as literal as the problem: AppleScript walks every window's tabs for a URL substring, sets that tab active, sets its window to index 1, then asks System Events to make Chrome frontmost, with delay 0.6 and delay 0.4 to let the window server catch up.

The flip side is the useful half. Once capture is live, Chrome pins the tab visible and the whole constraint evaporates — proven, not assumed: with a recording running, burying Chrome behind another app kept visibilityState: "visible" and hasFocus: false while the counter advanced in exact real time, 50 ticks over 5.0 seconds. The user can switch apps and keep working.

Say something before the screen goes red

Arming the click target throws a red overlay across the user's entire screen — position:fixed; inset:0; z-index:2147483647, 92% opaque — and then raise-chrome.sh yanks Chrome in front of whatever they were doing. You click that overlay. They don't know that.

"I had no idea I, as a human, was supposed to click that."

That is a verbatim user report, and it is why step 0 of the procedure is one line in chat before anything happens. Same reason the overlay's default label is worded at the person — "● Claude is starting a screen recording / No action needed — this overlay disappears in a moment" — rather than as the imperative "CLICK TO START RECORDING". Override the label if you like; keep that property.

Where it actually goes wrong: the click

Screenshot dimensions change between calls — 1456×840 then 1502×818 were observed for the same 1728px viewport — so any scale factor computed earlier silently misses. Always screenshot immediately before clicking and use that image's coordinates. armButton() covers the whole viewport for exactly this reason, and returns {armed, viewport, host} so you can check what you got.

If a click seems to do nothing, find out whether events are landing at all before changing any JS:

window.__clicks = [];
document.addEventListener('click', e => window.__clicks.push(
  [e.clientX, e.clientY, e.isTrusted]), true);
// state stays 'idle' + __clicks empty  → the click isn't reaching the page. Re-screenshot, re-click.
// state stays 'idle' + a TRUSTED click on some OTHER element → top-layer.

That second case is the sneaky one. An open <dialog> — or anything via showModal() / popover — paints in the top layer, above every z-index there is, so a target appended to <body> sits underneath it and the click hits the dialog. Seen on a session-detail modal, where the click logged cleanly as [864, 471, true, "SPAN"] while the recorder never left idle. armButton() now resolves its host as dialog[open] → [role="dialog"] → body and returns which one it used. When start() fails, diagnose() names the broken precondition directly, reading visibilityState, hasFocus and navigator.userActivation.

What actually records

No screenshot loop and no external recorder. The page records itself:

navigator.mediaDevices.getDisplayMedia({
  video: { frameRate: fps },      // 30 by default
  preferCurrentTab: true,         // ← removes the picker entirely
  selfBrowserSurface: 'include',
  audio: false,
});
new MediaRecorder(stream, { mimeType: 'video/webm;codecs=vp9',
                            videoBitsPerSecond: 8_000_000 }).start(250);

preferCurrentTab: true is the single flag that makes this humane — Chrome captures this tab with no chooser dialog, so no human has to pick a surface. start(250) passes a timeslice, flushing chunks every quarter second, so a crash loses a fraction of a second rather than the take. The arm target is removed the instant recording begins, so it never appears in frame. On stop(), the chunks become a Blob and a synthetic <a download> click puts it in ~/Downloads — a genuine browser download. The computer tool's save_to_disk does write a findable file now, but that is a screenshot: it covers stills, not a video stream.

The pointer nobody remembers until they watch it back

getDisplayMedia captures the system pointer, so wherever the agent's last click left the real mouse, it sits in every frame — usually parked on top of the UI, and unmissable on a Mac with a large cursor. There is no API to hide it. park() puts a 26px dot in the far bottom-right, returns its centre coordinates, and clicking it moves the OS pointer into the letterbox margin; the dot then removes itself and sets cap.parked.

And gate on cap.parked, not merely on RECORDING. The parking click is a screenshot plus a click, which is seconds — and every beat that starts before it is a beat with a mouse pointer wandering through it.

Then prove the file is real

ffprobe -v error -count_frames -select_streams v:0 \
  -show_entries stream=nb_read_frames,width,height -of default=nw=1 capture.webm
ffmpeg -y -loglevel error -i capture.webm -movflags faststart -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -vf "scale=trunc(iw/2)*2:trunc(ih/2)*2" capture.mp4

Hundreds of frames = real video. yuv420p and even dimensions aren't polish — without them QuickTime and Slack won't play the file at all. And MediaRecorder webm is variable-frame-rate, so frame-index extraction (-vf select='eq(n,N)') is unreliable: seek by time instead (-ss 7, -sseof -2.5).

The cursor is the narration

A recording has no click annotations of its own, so ui-narration's fake cursor, highlight box and click pulse are the explanation. It works with Route 1 and Route 2 alike. Three of its rules break a take rather than merely annoy:

  • Wait for everything. An early click is the most common way a take breaks.
  • Frame one element, click another when a card's centre sits over some other control — that's what the two arguments to focusAndClick are for.
  • Assert on geometry, never on "the call returned". A drag or resize that does nothing is indistinguishable from one that works — the handle is there, the wait resolves, nothing throws, and the rect comes back byte-identical. Measure before and after.

clap() is the one recorder-aware call: two white sync frames to align against the script's own clock, meaningless outside a recording. And captions ride along — if the flow calls __narrate.say(), a <name>.vtt is written beside the video, so one take ships burned-in or clean plus a sidecar.
